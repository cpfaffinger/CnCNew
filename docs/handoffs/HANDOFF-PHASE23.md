## Goal

60 Hz becomes the product rate for skirmish **and** campaign. Before that it is shown that the game at 60 Hz is *the same game* as at 30 Hz – not via hashes (they cannot match) but via measured game quantities. For this the acceptance tests are designed anew. After that every reference value is re-measured at 60 Hz.

## Honest status (starting point)

- After Phase 20 all time and rate values are converted, and times from the data lie on the 30 Hz grid of the original. **Not** dealt with is everything that is integrated per tick and changes with the step size (`docs/TICKRATE-AUDIT.md` §5 and the list from Phase 20): ground movement with turn limiting, threshold angle and arrival radius "one tick of travel"; winged flight and jets (lower bounds per tick, speed used as acceleration); projectiles (acceleration, re-alignment, gravity, detonation one step after the last point); toppling (Euler with bounce, multiplicative decay); multiplicative damping per frame (helicopter crash, neutron missile, train formation, train, barrier); deviating integration order (Battle Bus); turret traverse against a fixed angle; random draws per check.
- There is **no test** that compares two rates. All existing acceptances (reference match, stress test, AI table, campaign audit, objective driver, reachability, commands-only agent) are hashes or counts at 30 Hz with tick budgets that mean 30 Hz (`drive` 60,000 ticks, `audit --ticks 900`, `ai-match --max-ticks 45000`).
- After Phase 20 the skirmish AI thinks in game time, its results at 60 Hz are not assessed.

## Decisions that are fixed (`docs/MULTIPLAYER-PLAN.md` §2)

- Supported rates: 30, 60, 120. Product rate after this phase: 60. The rate remains a parameter of the session; 30 and 120 remain runnable and are permanently checked along by the rate-equivalence suite (that is the protection against new coupling).
- **After this phase golden hashes exist only for 60 Hz.** Two rates with a complete set of references each are not maintained.
- A saved state carries its rate and continues with it (Phase 20).
- **The escape "original grid" (Ausweg „Originalraster“):** logic that the original executes once per frame and that cannot sensibly be converted to a different step size may run on every (rate/30)-th tick. Every such place is counted, justified and kept in `docs/SIMULATION-CORE.md`; for movement, command acceptance and target acquisition the escape is not permitted (that is where the benefit of the higher rate lies).

## Brief (in this order)

1. **Reproduce the starting state.** Build, tests, manifest at 30 Hz, performance profile, determinism rows of your own machine.
2. **Design and build the rate-equivalence suite** (`tests/CnCNew.Simulation.Tests`, suite `RateEquivalence`, with real data via `--content-dir`; in addition `SimTool rate-compare` for individual cases). Every case sets up a small scene, runs it at 30, 60 and 120 Hz and compares **game quantities in seconds and world units** against a tolerance declared per quantity. The tolerances are a table in `docs/SIMULATION-CORE.md`; proposal: times from data exactly equal (they lie on the same grid), event times from movement ± 1 original frame, distances and end positions ± 0.5 %, rates over 60 s (damage, income) ± 1 %.
   - **Locomotion, automatically over all locomotors of the retail data:** time and distance to top speed, braking distance, time for 90°/180°, a drive over a fixed route with curves, arrival accuracy, damaged state.
   - **Flight:** take-off, cruise, turn, landing of the jets; helicopters; drop; parachute.
   - **Weapons, automatically over all weapon templates:** interval between shots, magazine and reloading, `PreAttackDelay`, firing stages, damage per 60 s on a standard target, range.
   - **Projectiles:** flight time and impact point per projectile module (guided, ballistic, arc), scatter on average.
   - **Turrets:** traverse time, fire clearance, return to the rest position, idle traverse.
   - **Damage and healing over time:** poison, fire, radiation, repair, self-healing, base regeneration.
   - **Economy and construction:** build time, production time, upgrade time, selling, one collector round trip, income per 5 minutes, black market/hacker/oil derrick cycles.
   - **Special powers and time modules:** cooldowns, lifetimes, slow death, toppling (duration and end position), mines, stealth and unstealth, vision and reveal.
   - **Scripts:** timers in seconds *and* in frames, counters, evaluation delay, sequence waiting times, `EndGameDelay`.
   - **AI:** decisions per game minute, time of the first barracks, of the first attack.
   The suite is supposed to show **many failures** on its first run – that is its purpose. The list of failures is the work backlog for step 3.
3. **Work off the causes until the suite passes.** Per cause: finding, correction, suite run, one commit. Rules:
   - **At 30 Hz the manifest stays unchanged** as long as the correction concerns only the scaling. Where the correction changes the behaviour at 30 Hz as well (e.g. a different integration order), that is a separate, named change with a rewritten manifest – only if there is no scaling way.
   - Additive quantities per frame: scale with 30/rate. Multiplicative factors per frame *f*: `f^(30/Rate)`, at 60 Hz the square root, at 120 Hz the fourth root (via `Fixed.Sqrt`, during catalogue building, with a test of the accuracy reached).
   - Thresholds that mean "one tick of travel" or "one tick of angle" (arrival radius, turret traverse reversal, rest threshold): express them as one original frame of travel or angle.
   - Events that occur "one step later" (detonation, firing state): one original frame later.
   - Random draws per check: after Phase 20 the check rhythm lies in game time; show that the number of draws per game time is equal.
   - The escape "original grid" only as defined above.
4. **Distribution comparison for what is chaotic.** A whole match is not comparable case by case. Therefore: every pairing of the AI table with 20 seeds at 30 and at 60 Hz; compared are the win rate per faction, the share of decided matches and the mean match duration in game minutes. The permissible deviation is defined and justified beforehand (calculate the sampling variance for 20 runs, do not estimate it). The same for the campaign with the objective driver: victory/defeat per map and time of the end script in game seconds.
5. **Performance gate at 60 Hz.** Repeat the profile from Phase 19 at 60 Hz: tick time (mean, 99 %, maximum), catch-up speed as a multiple of real time, garbage collection, cost of the game layer per tick in the client, frame rate of the client in an 8-player match. Target values from `docs/handoffs/HANDOFF-PHASE19.md`; the **measured catch-up speed at 60 Hz** is a figure that Phases 26 and 27 rely on. If a target value is missed, first work off the cost centre (rules of Phase 19), then switch.
6. **Switch.** Default rate 60 in sessions, client, tools and tests. Compute all tick budgets that mean game time from game time (objective driver 60,000 ticks at 30 Hz ≙ 2,000 s; `audit --ticks 900` ≙ 30 s; `ai-match --max-ticks 45000` ≙ 25 min; stress test 216,000 ticks ≙ 2 h) – in future the tools take `--seconds`/`--minutes`, `--ticks` remains as a raw value. **[Addendum 9 October 2026: the statement on the stress test holds only for 30 Hz. The stress test counts steps and is deliberately not converted – 216,000 steps are one hour of game time at 60 Hz, with a new final hash; `docs/TICKRATE-MIGRATION.md` §15.5.2.]**
7. **Re-measure everything at 60 Hz and rewrite the manifest:** reference match, stress test, AI table (all maps and pairings, `docs/SIMULATION-CORE.md` §19 completely new), `skirmish-sweep` over all 64 maps, the 8-player runs, campaign (`drive` victory/defeat per map, `audit` deterministic and equal after save/load, `reachability`, commands-only agent), the scene audits (`audit-scenarios.ps1`), the determinism matrix. Every number from a run, each campaign individually, background runs with a bounded timeout.
8. **Formats.** Check all tick-related options in saved states (`MissionSnapshot`: `maxTicks`, `presentationLatencyTicks`, `combatIntervalTicks`): store them as time or interpret them with the saved rate when loading; increase versions where bytes change.
9. **Prepare the visual check.** Extend the list from Phase 21 (effects, muzzle flash, suspension, mission intro) by what this phase has changed (driving behaviour, projectile trajectories, turret movement), as a checklist in `docs/DEMO-FINDINGS.md`; make it startable with the demo scripts at 60 Hz. Only a human can tick it off.
10. **Bring the docs fully up to date.** Every statement "30 Hz", every tick count and every reference value in `README.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md`, `docs/IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md`, `docs/SIMULATION-CORE.md`, `docs/GAME-RULES.md`, `docs/SCRIPT-ENGINE.md`, `docs/CAMPAIGN-AND-SAVEGAMES.md`, the `SIM-*.md`, `CLAUDE.md`: update it or mark it as historical (30 Hz). ## Bottlenecks and showstoppers of this phase

- **Movement is the hardest part.** Path following, avoiding stationary units (replanning, look-ahead), collision and formations react to the step. Small differences per tick lead to different paths. The suite therefore checks individual quantities tightly and whole drives loosely; the distribution comparison catches the rest.
- **Chaos is not an error, a systematic offset is.** If a faction reliably does better at 60 Hz, a rate or duration is scaled wrongly somewhere. Then look for the first diverging quantity with `SimTool ai-match --trace` and `rate-compare`.
- **Running time of the re-measurement.** At 60 Hz the tick count of every run doubles. Without the performance work from Phase 19, step 7 takes many hours.
- **Campaign scripts** are written for 30 frames per second (frame counters, tight temporal sequences). Every map whose outcome or end time shifts is examined individually.

## Non-negotiable rules

`CLAUDE.md` and `docs/MULTIPLAYER-PLAN.md` §6. For this phase: one cause per commit; every tolerance is defined and justified before the run, never adjusted to the result afterwards; "passes" means at 30, 60 **and** 120 Hz.

The final report contains the table of the causes worked off, the places of the escape "original grid", old and new reference values with commit hashes – and explicitly that no human has seen the game at 60 Hz as long as that has not happened.
